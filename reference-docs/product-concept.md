# PART I: COMPETITION CONCEPT APPLICATION

## 1. Executive Summary

Agriculture is one of the most important contributors to Nigeria's economy and a major source of livelihood for rural households. However, smallholder farmers continue to face challenges that prevent them from turning their agricultural production into sustainable income.

Many farmers struggle to find reliable buyers, negotiate favourable prices, arrange affordable transportation, access genuine agricultural inputs and obtain appropriate financing. These challenges are particularly damaging for farmers producing perishable crops, where delays in finding buyers or arranging transportation can result in deterioration, distress sales and avoidable financial losses.

The problem is not simply that farmers lack access to markets. It is that the different services required to successfully produce, sell and deliver agricultural products remain fragmented and difficult for many underserved farmers to access.

**Tongric is a multilingual agricultural commerce and fulfilment platform designed to connect smallholder farmers directly with buyers, coordinate agricultural transportation and provide access to essential agricultural services and financing through one accessible digital platform.**

Tongric will enable farmers to advertise available produce, discover potential buyers, negotiate transactions and arrange transportation through registered logistics providers. Buyers, including food processors, wholesalers, retailers, restaurants and other businesses, will be able to discover agricultural produce and source directly from farmers.

The platform will also introduce farmer-to-farmer agricultural exchange, verified agricultural input suppliers and access to financing through partnerships with licensed financial institutions.

A defining feature of Tongric is its commitment to local-language accessibility. Farmers will be able to navigate the platform in supported Nigerian languages through a simple interface designed for users with different levels of digital literacy. The platform will also be developed with low-data usage and assisted onboarding in mind.

Tongric will initially pilot in Benue State, where agricultural production is significant and research has documented challenges involving storage, packaging, transportation and post-harvest losses.

The initial product will focus on three interconnected activities:

1. Connecting farmers with genuine buyers.
2. Coordinating transportation and delivery of agricultural produce.
3. Making these services accessible through a multilingual, low-data digital platform.

Additional agricultural services will be introduced progressively.

**Tongric's vision is to make agricultural market participation easier, more transparent and more profitable for smallholder farmers, irrespective of their location, language or existing market connections.**

## 2. The Problem We Are Solving

### 2.1 Smallholder farmers are essential to Nigeria's food system, but face structural barriers

According to the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), agriculture contributed approximately 28% of Nigeria's GDP between 2021 and 2024 and employed approximately 40% of the country's labour force.

The United States International Trade Administration's 2025 Nigeria Agriculture Sector report estimates that approximately 80% of Nigerian farmers are smallholders and that they account for around 90% of agricultural production.

These figures demonstrate the importance of smallholder farmers to Nigeria's economy and food supply.

Despite their contribution, many smallholder farmers face difficulties accessing the commercial and support services required to improve their livelihoods.

Their challenges include:

- Limited access to reliable and profitable markets.
- Dependence on informal market networks and intermediaries.
- Poor coordination between produce availability and transportation.
- Inadequate access to storage and preservation facilities.
- Limited access to genuine agricultural inputs.
- Difficulty accessing appropriate agricultural financing.
- Limited access to trusted agricultural information.
- Language, connectivity and digital literacy barriers.

**The central problem is the fragmented agricultural service ecosystem that makes it difficult for smallholder farmers to move from production to profitable market participation.**

### 2.2 Farmers struggle to find buyers at the right time

Consider a smallholder farmer who harvests 500 kilograms of fresh tomatoes.

The farmer needs to sell the tomatoes quickly because the produce is perishable.

The farmer contacts available traders and middlemen, but the offers received are lower than expected.

The farmer attempts to find alternative buyers, but has limited access to businesses outside the immediate local market.

Even if another buyer is identified, transportation must still be arranged.

The farmer may eventually accept a significantly reduced price because the cost of waiting becomes greater than the benefit of negotiating.

In another situation, the farmer may fail to sell part of the produce before it deteriorates.

This is a market-access problem, a coordination problem and an income problem.

It is important to recognise that intermediaries are not inherently harmful. Many provide valuable services, including aggregation, market discovery, transportation and financing. Tongric is not designed to eliminate useful intermediaries. It is designed to give farmers more choices, greater transparency and direct access to additional buyers and service providers.

### 2.3 Evidence of post-harvest challenges in Benue State

A 2024 study by Yanjoh and colleagues examined post-harvest losses among 200 rural farmers across Benue State's agricultural zones.

The study focused on tomatoes, mangoes, pepper and cashew.

Its findings identified:

| Reported issue | Percentage |
|---|---:|
| Lack of good storage facilities | 45% |
| Poor packaging | 45% |
| Poor transportation networks | 28% |
| Reduced income identified as an effect of post-harvest losses | 33% |
| Reduced produce quality identified as an effect | 31% |

The study demonstrates that post-harvest challenges involve more than the physical handling of produce. Storage, transportation and market coordination all contribute to the problem.

An earlier study of 120 fruit and vegetable producers in Gboko Local Government Area reported that 57.5% experienced produce losses associated with the non-availability of buyers.

Although these studies are not statewide prevalence estimates, they provide local evidence supporting the need to investigate and address post-harvest challenges in Benue.

### 2.4 Transportation is an important missing link

Access to a buyer does not guarantee a successful sale.

Farmers and buyers still need a reliable way to move agricultural products from farms or collection points to their destinations.

Transportation challenges include:

- Difficulty locating available vehicles.
- Unclear transportation charges.
- Limited access to transport providers willing to carry smaller quantities.
- Poor coordination between harvest and vehicle availability.
- Difficulty monitoring delivery progress.
- Lack of reliable delivery confirmation.

For perishable crops, delays can affect product quality and market value.

Tongric therefore addresses not only buyer discovery but also the transportation required to complete agricultural transactions.

### 2.5 Farmers face barriers to affordable financing

Farmers require capital for seeds, fertilisers, labour, equipment and other production expenses.

However, access to appropriate credit remains limited for many smallholder farmers.

According to the Development Bank of Nigeria's 2025 agricultural finance analysis, agricultural lending represented approximately 6.30% of total private-sector credit in 2025, while agriculture contributed approximately 27.55% of GDP.

This difference illustrates the gap between the economic importance of agriculture and its share of formal private-sector lending.

Tongric proposes to create a more accessible digital pathway through which farmers can discover financing opportunities and apply through licensed financial institutions.

### 2.6 Digital services remain inaccessible to many rural users

According to GSMA's 2025 State of Mobile Internet Connectivity report, its 2024 Nigerian consumer survey found:

| Mobile connectivity indicator | Rural population | Urban population |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mobile phone ownership | 90% | 95% |
| Awareness of mobile internet | 78% | 96% |
| Ownership of an internet-enabled handset | 39% | 73% |
| Mobile internet adoption | 28% | 61% |
| Daily mobile internet use | 23% | 53% |

These findings reveal an important design consideration.

Although mobile phone ownership is relatively high, access to internet-enabled devices and actual mobile internet use are substantially lower in rural communities.

A solution that assumes every farmer has a smartphone, sufficient data and the ability to navigate a conventional application risks excluding many of its intended beneficiaries.

Tongric will therefore prioritise affordability, lightweight design, language accessibility and assisted community onboarding.

### 2.7 The specific problem Tongric addresses

Tongric focuses on the gap between agricultural production and successful market fulfilment.

Smallholder farmers need a practical way to:

- Find buyers beyond their immediate market networks.
- Compare available offers.
- Agree on transactions transparently.
- Arrange transportation without unnecessary delays.
- Confirm that their produce reaches the intended buyer.
- Access additional agricultural services through trusted partners.

Existing agricultural services may address individual parts of this process, but Tongric proposes to coordinate these activities through one accessible platform designed around smallholder farmers.

## 3. Our Solution: Tongric

### 3.1 What Tongric does

Tongric is a multilingual digital agricultural marketplace and fulfilment platform that connects smallholder farmers directly with agricultural buyers and coordinates the services required to complete produce transactions.

The platform brings together five principal participant groups:

1. Farmers.
2. Agricultural buyers and businesses.
3. Transportation providers.
4. Agricultural input suppliers.
5. Financial service institutions.

Tongric provides each participant with relevant tools through a central digital platform.

### 3.2 How the platform works

Tongric is built around one central transaction journey:

**Farmer lists produce → Buyer discovers produce → Both parties agree → Transportation is arranged → Produce is delivered → Transaction is confirmed.**

This is the core workflow that the first version of Tongric will demonstrate.

#### Example: How Tongric helps a tomato farmer

A farmer in Gboko harvests 500 kilograms of tomatoes.

**Without Tongric:**

The farmer depends on existing market contacts to find buyers. If available buyers offer unfavourable prices, the farmer may spend valuable time looking for alternatives. Transportation must be arranged separately, and the farmer may eventually accept a reduced price or lose part of the produce.

**With Tongric:**

1. The farmer opens Tongric and selects their preferred language.
2. The farmer lists 500 kilograms of tomatoes, their location, expected quality, asking price and availability.
3. A registered food processor in Makurdi searches for tomatoes and discovers the listing.
4. The buyer contacts the farmer and negotiates the quantity and price.
5. Both parties agree to purchase 500 kilograms.
6. The buyer or farmer searches Tongric for an available transport provider.
7. A transport provider submits a quotation.
8. The parties agree on transportation arrangements.
9. The produce is collected and delivery milestones are recorded.
10. The buyer confirms delivery and the transaction is completed.

The farmer has gained access to an additional buyer and a coordinated delivery process without depending exclusively on their existing market network.

The platform does not guarantee that every listed product will sell or that every transaction will be more profitable. Its value is creating additional opportunities and reducing avoidable coordination barriers.

## 4. Tongric's Core Innovative Solutions

Tongric's innovation lies in combining agricultural market access, fulfilment coordination and inclusive digital service delivery in a single system.

### 4.1 Innovation One: Direct Farmer-to-Buyer Agricultural Marketplace

**The problem:** Farmers often depend on limited market networks and may struggle to discover buyers beyond their immediate locations.

**Our solution:** Tongric creates a digital marketplace where farmers can list produce and businesses can discover available agricultural products directly from their sources.

#### How it works

Farmers register and create produce listings containing:

- Product name.
- Quantity available.
- Asking price or negotiable price.
- Location.
- Harvest or availability date.
- Quality description.
- Product photographs.
- Preferred collection or delivery arrangements.

Buyers register as businesses or organisations and can search for produce based on:

- Crop type.
- Available quantity.
- Location.
- Price.
- Availability date.
- Other relevant specifications.

A buyer can contact a farmer, submit an offer or initiate a purchase request.

Farmers can review available offers and accept, reject or negotiate.

Once an agreement is reached, Tongric creates an order record.

#### Who benefits?

- Farmers gain additional market opportunities.
- Buyers discover additional suppliers.
- Food processors can source agricultural raw materials.
- Retailers and wholesalers can discover available produce.
- Agricultural businesses can improve supplier discovery.

#### What makes this valuable?

Tongric makes market discovery more accessible and provides a structured environment for farmers and buyers to negotiate and record transactions.

### 4.2 Innovation Two: Integrated Agricultural Logistics and Delivery Coordination

**The problem:** Farmers may identify buyers but struggle to arrange transportation quickly and affordably.

**Our solution:** Tongric integrates registered transportation providers into the agricultural marketplace.

#### How it works

Transportation companies and individual transport operators can create provider profiles.

They provide information about:

- Vehicle types.
- Carrying capacity.
- Operating locations.
- Delivery routes.
- Availability.
- Pricing.
- Contact details.

When a farmer and buyer agree on a transaction, they can request transportation through Tongric.

The platform displays relevant providers based on their service areas and vehicle capacities.

The parties can compare quotations and agree on the provider.

A delivery record is then created, linked to the agricultural order.

#### Delivery tracking

The initial system will support milestone-based tracking:

1. Transportation requested.
2. Provider confirmed.
3. Produce collected.
4. Shipment dispatched.
5. Produce delivered.
6. Buyer confirms receipt.

Future versions can introduce GPS-enabled tracking and route optimisation.

#### Practical example

A buyer in Makurdi purchases 1,000 kilograms of cassava from a farmer in Gboko.

Instead of searching independently for a truck, the buyer or farmer checks available transportation providers on Tongric.

A registered operator with sufficient capacity provides a quotation.

Once accepted, the provider receives the delivery request and updates the shipment status as the cassava moves to the buyer.

Tongric creates a record of the order and its delivery.

#### What makes this innovative?

The platform coordinates buyer discovery and transportation instead of treating them as unrelated activities.

This is especially relevant for perishable produce, where delays can reduce quality and market value.

Tongric will also allow farmers to discover existing storage and aggregation facilities where available. It will connect users to existing infrastructure rather than claim to own or operate facilities that it does not have.

### 4.3 Innovation Three: Local-Language-First Agricultural Access

**The problem:** Conventional digital applications often assume that users can comfortably read and interact in English.

For farmers in underserved communities, language and digital literacy can create significant barriers.

**Our solution:** Tongric will support multiple Nigerian languages and provide a simplified interface that allows farmers to use the platform in a language they understand.

#### Proposed languages

- English.
- Tiv.
- Hausa.
- Yoruba.
- Igbo.
- Fulfulde (Fulani).
- Kanuri.

The initial pilot will prioritise languages according to the communities selected.

#### How it works

During onboarding, users choose their preferred language.

The application loads the corresponding translated interface.

Language settings will remain accessible so users can change their preference whenever necessary.

Translation will cover:

- Navigation.
- Registration.
- Produce listings.
- Order management.
- Transaction notifications.
- Error messages.
- Important instructions.
- Financial information.

The language system will use a central translation management structure so additional languages can be added without rebuilding the application.

All translations will be reviewed by competent native speakers, especially agricultural terminology and financial disclosures.

We will not assume that Google Translate supports every proposed language. Where reliable automated translation is unavailable, we will use locally validated translation resources.

#### Additional accessibility features

- Large and clear interface elements.
- Simple terminology.
- Minimal steps for common tasks.
- Compressed images.
- Low-data application design.
- Assisted registration through community agents.
- Future voice-assisted interaction.
- SMS and WhatsApp notifications where appropriate.

#### Why this matters

Tongric is not designed only for farmers who are already comfortable with digital technology.

It is designed to make agricultural digital services more accessible to farmers who have historically faced barriers to using conventional applications.

### 4.4 Innovation Four: Farmer-to-Farmer Agricultural Exchange

**The problem:** Farmers may possess agricultural resources that other farmers need but lack an organised way to discover and negotiate exchanges.

Informal barter arrangements already exist within parts of the Nigerian agricultural ecosystem.

**Our solution:** Tongric will provide a dedicated exchange marketplace that allows farmers to trade agricultural products, seeds and other permitted resources.

#### How it works

A farmer creates an exchange listing indicating:

- What they have.
- The quantity available.
- What they want in exchange.
- Their location.
- Product quality or specifications.

Other farmers can search and discover suitable exchange opportunities.

They can contact each other, negotiate an exchange ratio and agree on fulfilment.

The platform can create a digital agreement and provide access to transportation services.

#### Example

A farmer in Benue has agricultural seeds but needs a specified quantity of another crop.

Another farmer in a different state has the required crop but needs the seeds.

Both farmers discover each other through Tongric, negotiate the exchange and agree on delivery arrangements.

The platform records the agreement and provides a transaction history.

#### What makes this valuable?

It creates a digital channel for an existing agricultural trading practice and expands the ways farmers can access resources without relying exclusively on cash transactions.

The feature will initially be secondary to the main produce marketplace.

### 4.5 Innovation Five: Access to Agricultural Financing

**The problem:** Smallholder farmers often struggle to access suitable financing because of limited formal financial records, collateral requirements and difficulty discovering appropriate financial products.

**Our solution:** Tongric will provide a central digital access point through which farmers can discover and apply for agricultural financing offered by licensed partner institutions.

#### How it works

Financial institutions register as service partners and provide details of their financing products.

Farmers can:

- View available financing options.
- Understand eligibility requirements.
- Review interest rates and repayment terms.
- Submit applications.
- Upload required documents.
- Track application progress.
- Receive application notifications.

Applications are transmitted to the relevant financial institution for assessment.

The financial institution remains responsible for credit assessment, approval, disbursement and repayment administration.

Tongric will not independently act as a lender or promise loan approval.

#### The transaction-history opportunity

As farmers complete transactions through Tongric, the platform can build consented records of their commercial activities.

These records may include:

- Produce supplied.
- Quantity sold.
- Transaction frequency.
- Sales value.
- Buyer relationships.
- Delivery history.

With farmer consent and appropriate agreements, participating financial institutions may consider these records as supplementary information during credit assessment.

This does not automatically establish creditworthiness or replace the lender's underwriting process.

#### What makes this valuable?

Tongric can connect market participation with access to financial services, helping create a pathway through which farmers build more visible records of their economic activities.

### 4.6 Innovation Six: Verified Agricultural Input Marketplace

**The problem:** Farmers may struggle to identify genuine seeds, fertilisers and other agricultural inputs at affordable prices.

**Our solution:** Tongric will provide a digital directory and marketplace of agricultural input suppliers.

#### How it works

Suppliers register and provide:

- Business information.
- Product categories.
- Product descriptions.
- Prices.
- Locations.
- Relevant documentation.

Tongric will conduct appropriate supplier checks before displaying verified status.

Farmers can discover suppliers, compare available products and make enquiries or purchases.

The platform will provide mechanisms for users to report suspected counterfeit products or misleading listings.

Verification will be based on documented checks, not merely account registration.

#### What makes this valuable?

It gives farmers another route to discover agricultural inputs and helps improve visibility of supplier information.

### 4.7 Future Innovation: Agricultural Intelligence and Land Suitability

Tongric's longer-term development includes agricultural intelligence tools.

Farmers may eventually provide land location, soil information and photographs.

The platform could combine these inputs with validated soil datasets, satellite observations, weather information and agricultural research to provide preliminary land suitability and crop recommendations.

Potential capabilities include:

- Preliminary soil assessment.
- Crop suitability recommendations.
- Seasonal planting information.
- Weather and climate insights.
- Agricultural risk alerts.
- Satellite-based land monitoring.

This feature will require scientific validation and should not be presented as a definitive soil laboratory replacement.

It is a future capability, not part of the initial MVP.

## 5. Why Tongric Is Different

Tongric is not claiming to be the first digital agricultural marketplace in Nigeria.

Existing agricultural platforms have already demonstrated the potential of digital farmer services, financing and market connections.

Tongric's differentiation is its integrated approach to the complete agricultural transaction, with particular emphasis on underserved farmers and perishable produce.

| Existing market challenge | Tongric's proposed response |
|---|---|
| Farmers have limited buyer discovery | Direct digital farmer-to-buyer marketplace |
| Farmers find buyers but struggle with delivery | Integrated transportation coordination |
| Farmers have limited access to transaction information | Digital orders and delivery records |
| Conventional applications may exclude local-language users | Multilingual interface and assisted onboarding |
| Agricultural exchanges are often informal | Structured farmer-to-farmer exchange listings |
| Farmers struggle to discover financing | Access portal for licensed financial partners |
| Farmers struggle to identify genuine inputs | Verified agricultural supplier directory |

**Our core innovation is the coordination of agricultural commerce and fulfilment through an inclusive digital platform designed around the realities of smallholder farmers.**

This combination is the product proposition we intend to validate.

## 7. Initial Deployment: Benue State

Benue State is proposed as Tongric's initial pilot location because of its agricultural importance and documented post-harvest challenges.

The Benue State Government identifies the state as a major producer of yam, cassava, rice, maize, soybeans, fruits and vegetables.

The Nigerian Investment Promotion Commission identifies Benue as Nigeria's leading yam-producing state and reports its significant contribution to national soybean production.

The 2024 Benue post-harvest study provides additional evidence of storage, packaging and transportation challenges among sampled rural farmers.

Tongric will begin in one or two selected farming communities in Benue.

The pilot will test whether Tongric can successfully connect farmers with buyers and coordinate produce delivery.

We will expand to additional crops and locations only after validating the initial model.

## 9. Affordability, Accessibility and Adoption

Tongric's adoption strategy is based on the principle that a platform is only useful when its intended beneficiaries can actually access and use it.

### 9.1 Affordable participation

Basic farmer registration and produce listing will be free.

We will avoid mandatory subscriptions that prevent smallholder farmers from participating.

Any transaction charges will be transparent and communicated before an agreement is completed.

### 9.2 Local-language accessibility

Farmers will be able to use supported local languages.

The initial pilot languages will be selected according to the needs of the communities involved.

### 9.3 Low-data design

The application will:

- Minimise unnecessary page requests.
- Compress images.
- Avoid heavy animations.
- Cache frequently used information.
- Provide clear network status.
- Support retrying failed submissions.

### 9.4 Assisted onboarding

Tongric will explore partnerships with farmer cooperatives, agricultural associations and community agents.

These partners can help farmers:

- Register.
- Create their first produce listings.
- Understand buyer enquiries.
- Use transportation services.
- Access support.

This will help users who do not have sufficient digital confidence to use the platform independently.

### 9.5 Trust and transparency

The platform will introduce appropriate verification procedures for farmers, buyers, transportation providers and suppliers.

Transaction records and feedback mechanisms will help improve accountability.

### 9.6 Future access channels

Tongric will investigate:

- SMS notifications.
- WhatsApp communication.
- USSD-based access, subject to commercial and technical feasibility.
- Voice-assisted interaction.

These features will be prioritised according to actual user needs and available resources.

## 10. Business Model and Financial Sustainability

Tongric will adopt a multi-sided marketplace business model.

The objective is to generate revenue from value created for buyers, businesses and service providers while keeping essential farmer access affordable.

### 10.1 Marketplace transaction fees

Tongric may charge a small, transparent fee on successfully completed produce transactions.

The recommended initial approach is a buyer-side fee rather than charging farmers simply to list produce.

Example:

A buyer purchases agricultural produce worth ₦500,000.

If the agreed platform fee is 1%:

- Transaction value: ₦500,000.
- Platform fee: ₦5,000.
- Gross Tongric revenue: ₦5,000.

This is an illustrative calculation, not a validated market price.

Actual fees will be determined through buyer interviews and willingness-to-pay testing.

### 10.2 Logistics coordination fees

Transportation providers may pay a referral or coordination fee on successfully completed bookings.

Alternatively, a transparent service fee may be agreed for logistics coordination.

### 10.3 Institutional procurement services

Larger businesses may pay for additional sourcing services, including:

- Bulk produce discovery.
- Supplier coordination.
- Aggregation support.
- Procurement records.
- Dedicated sourcing assistance.

### 10.4 Agricultural input commissions

Tongric may earn a commission from verified suppliers on successfully completed transactions.

### 10.5 Financial services partnerships

Licensed financial institutions may compensate Tongric for permitted customer acquisition or application support services under formal agreements and applicable regulations.

### 10.6 Future premium services

As adoption grows, Tongric may introduce optional services such as:

- Advanced procurement dashboards.
- Agricultural market intelligence.
- Business analytics.
- Cooperative management tools.
- Institutional reporting.

### Revenue model summary

| Revenue stream | Priority |
|---|---|
| Produce marketplace transaction fees | High |
| Logistics coordination | High |
| Institutional procurement services | High |
| Agricultural input commissions | Medium |
| Financial services partnerships | Medium |
| Premium agricultural intelligence | Long term |

### Sustainability principle

Tongric will not rely solely on grants or donor funding.

The long-term objective is to build a commercially sustainable business through transaction revenue, business services and strategic partnerships while keeping basic farmer participation accessible.

# PART II: PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT BLUEPRINT

## 11. Product Vision and Development Principles

The first version of Tongric must demonstrate a complete and useful agricultural transaction.

We will not attempt to build every proposed feature simultaneously.

The initial objective is to prove that a farmer can list available produce, discover a buyer, agree on a sale, arrange transportation and confirm delivery through Tongric.

The product will follow five principles:

1. Farmer-first usability.
2. Low-cost and low-data access.
3. Trustworthy transactions.
4. Modular development.
5. Evidence-driven expansion.

## 12. Product User Roles

Tongric will initially support five principal account types.

### 12.1 Farmer

Can:

- Register.
- Select a language.
- Manage a profile.
- Create produce listings.
- Edit and deactivate listings.
- Receive buyer enquiries.
- Review offers.
- Negotiate transactions.
- Confirm orders.
- Request transportation.
- Track deliveries.
- View transaction history.

### 12.2 Buyer

Can:

- Register a business profile.
- Submit required verification information.
- Search produce.
- Filter listings.
- Contact farmers.
- Submit offers.

## 13. Core User Journeys

### Journey A: Farmer lists and sells produce

1. Farmer visits Tongric.
2. Selects preferred language.
3. Creates an account.
4. Completes profile information.
5. Opens the produce marketplace.
6. Selects Add Produce.
7. Enters crop, quantity, price, location and availability.
8. Uploads a photograph if available.
9. Publishes the listing.
10. Receives buyer enquiries.
11. Negotiates and accepts an offer.
12. Confirms the order.
13. Arranges transportation.
14. Tracks delivery milestones.
15. Confirms transaction completion.

### Journey B: Buyer sources agricultural produce

1. Buyer registers.
2. Completes business profile.
3. Searches available produce.
4. Filters by crop, quantity and location.
5. Reviews farmer listings.
6. Submits an offer or purchase request.
7. Negotiates with the farmer.
8. Confirms the order.
9. Arranges transportation.
10. Receives the produce.
11. Confirms delivery.

### Journey C: Transport provider completes a delivery

1. Provider registers.
2. Submits vehicle and service information.
3. Completes verification.
4. Views available delivery requests.
5. Submits a quotation.
6. Receives an accepted booking.
7. Confirms collection.

1. Farmer opens the exchange section.
2. Creates an exchange listing.
3. States available resources.
4. Specifies desired resources.
5. Searches matching listings.
6. Contacts another farmer.
7. Negotiates exchange terms.

## 14. Functional Requirements

The following requirements define what the development team should implement.

### Module 1: Authentication and account management

**FR-001:** Users must be able to create accounts.

**FR-002:** Users must select an account role.

## 18. Important Order and Delivery Logic

The marketplace must distinguish between an enquiry, an offer, an accepted agreement and a completed transaction.

A recommended order lifecycle is:

- Draft.
- Pending acceptance.
- Negotiation.
- Confirmed.
- Transportation requested.
- Transport assigned.
- Collected.
- In transit.
- Delivered.
- Completed.
- Cancelled.
- Disputed.

Not every order must pass through every delivery status. Collection arrangements may differ.

The system must record who made important status changes and when they occurred.

### Transaction integrity

A farmer should not accidentally have the same quantity sold to multiple buyers.

The system should reserve agreed quantities when orders are confirmed and prevent available quantities from becoming negative.

Where a listing contains 500 kilograms and a buyer agrees to purchase 200 kilograms, the remaining available quantity should become 300 kilograms, subject to the order's confirmation rules.

This is a critical marketplace requirement.

## 19. Payment Strategy

The initial MVP should not attempt to operate as an unlicensed payment intermediary.

The first release can record agreed transaction values and payment arrangements without holding customer funds.

Where digital payments are introduced, Tongric should integrate an appropriately authorised payment service provider.

The platform must clearly communicate:

- Whether payment is made directly to the seller.
- Whether payment is processed through a third-party provider.
- Whether any transaction fee applies.
- Whether refunds or disputes are supported.

Escrow or custody of customer funds should only be considered after obtaining appropriate legal and regulatory guidance and establishing a compliant partnership.

## 34. Why Tongric Matters

Nigeria's smallholder farmers are essential to the country's food system.

Farmers need access to buyers, affordable transportation, reliable agricultural services and appropriate financing.

Tongric addresses these challenges through an integrated agricultural commerce and fulfilment platform.

Beginning in Benue State, Tongric will validate its approach through a focused pilot before expanding into other agricultural communities.

**Tongric is building more than a digital marketplace. It is building a more accessible connection between the people who produce food and the businesses and markets that need it.**
